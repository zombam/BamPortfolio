Who

A fine artist who
learned to write code

I'm Bam — a London-based Creative Technologist and motion designer originally from Bangkok, Thailand. I hold an M.Sc. in Creative Computing from the University of the Arts London (Distinction, 2025), and a B.A. in Fine & Applied Arts from Chulalongkorn University (2nd Class Honours, 2019).

My background is unusual: I trained first as a visual artist, then spent six years in commercial design studios before returning to research. That sequence shapes everything I make — I approach code with a designer's eye for composition, and I approach design with an engineer's instinct for systems.

I have delivered over twelve interactive and generative projects from initial concept to on-site execution, across installation, broadcast, UX/UI, and physical computing.

What

Specialising in
real-time systems
& embodied AI

My technical practice centres on TouchDesigner, Python APIs, and generative AI pipelines (ComfyUI, Stable Diffusion, CNNs). I build systems that transform complex data — ocean readings, microplastic datasets, human motion — into experiences that can be felt rather than merely read.

My UAL thesis, The H.A.N.D., investigated embodied AI through a soft-robotic system inspired by the decentralised nervous system of the octopus. Each silicone digit operates its own state logic, communicating laterally rather than reporting to a central brain — producing behaviour that visitors consistently describe as alive.

On the design side, I've led UX/UI across web, mobile, and app projects, and produced motion graphics for broadcast clients including AIS Thailand. I work comfortably across Figma, the Adobe Creative Suite, Houdini, Blender, and Unity.

Why

Making the
invisible tangible

The thread running through every project I make is a single question: how do you make something abstract — a biological principle, a dataset, an ecological crisis — feel physically present in a room?

Coral Bleaching grew coral from hand gestures and let visitors watch it die. Marine Pulse turned ocean wave data into the breath of a glowing globe. The H.A.N.D. translated a neuroscience paper into a silicone artifact that seems to notice you.

I'm drawn to work that sits at the boundary between science and sensation — where the rigour of a system produces an experience that is genuinely surprising. I believe the most important question a creative technologist can ask is not "can this be built?" but "what should this feel like?"

Intellectual territory

Currently
thinking about

The territory I'm most interested in is the space where biology and computation genuinely borrow from each other — not as metaphor, but as engineering principle.

The octopus has been a recurring reference because its nervous system solves problems that distributed computing is still working on: fault tolerance, local adaptation, and the ability to act without a clear picture of the whole.

Research interest
Embodied Intelligence
How physical form can be a computational medium — where the material itself processes information before it reaches any digital system.
Technical interest
Distributed Finite State Machines
Architectures where intelligence is peripheral, not central. The H.A.N.D. was a first experiment. Scaling this to spatial environments is the next question.
Design interest
Interaction as Ecological Argument
Using interactive media to make environmental data emotionally legible — Coral Bleaching and Marine Pulse were both attempts at this. There is much more to explore.
